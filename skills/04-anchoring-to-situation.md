# 04 — Anchoring to a Situation

## When to fire
Provider has shared a general impression but no specific event. Examples: "He's not very collaborative", "She tends to overpromise", "They're often unclear in meetings".

## Purpose
Bring the conversation down from abstraction to a concrete moment that the recipient can actually engage with.

## Behaviour
- Ask for a specific instance, even a small one
- Offer scaffolds if the provider draws a blank ("a particular meeting? a recent project?")
- Don't accept "always", "often", or "usually" as the situation
- Once anchored, confirm and invite them to tell you what happened

## Probes

- "Can you think of a specific time when this happened?"
- "Is there a particular moment that stands out — even a small one?"
- "When did you notice this most recently?"
- "Was there a specific meeting, project, or interaction where this came up?"

## If provider continues to generalise

- "Let's try to find just one example to start with. Even something small."
- "If I asked you to pick the most recent time you noticed this, what would come to mind?"
- "Sometimes the easiest way is to think back over the last week or two — was there a moment that fits this pattern?"

## Once anchored, confirm

> Okay — so we're focusing on [meeting/event/moment]. Tell me what happened.

## Avoid
- Accepting "always" or "every time" as a substitute for a specific instance. Acknowledge that the behavior might be prevalent, but ask for concrete examples to make the feedback effective (see skill 02).
- Stacking multiple probes in one turn (one question at a time)
- Moving on without an anchor — the rest of the SBI depends on it
