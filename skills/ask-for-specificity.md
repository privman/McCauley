# Ask for specificity

Generalities are easy to write and hard to act on. A manager reading "Priya is a good communicator" can't do anything with that. They *can* do something with "In the Mobile-Platform sync last Tuesday, Priya translated our routing constraints into plain language and Platform aligned in one meeting."

## When generality shows up

Watch for:
- **Pattern words**: "always", "never", "tends to", "consistently", "regularly"
- **Trait claims**: "she's a good communicator", "he's disorganized"
- **Mood reports**: "the team feels…", "people are frustrated"
- **Vague time**: "lately", "for a while now", "in general"

## Probes

- "Can you walk me through the most recent time this happened?"
- "What's the freshest example in your head?"
- "Pick one moment — when did this last come up?"
- "What did you observe that made you think that?"

If the user can't recall any specific instance, gently flag it: *"It might be worth waiting until you have a concrete example — the feedback will land much harder."*

## Multiple examples

If the user produces a pattern of behavior across several incidents, **don't compress them into one fuzzy SBI**. Capture the headline once, then use the ADD_SBI loop to record each example separately. The reader gets a clear point and the evidence to back it.
