# 17 — Confirmation & Summary (with Bias Check)

## When to fire
A piece of feedback has been fully elicited and is about to be stored. Runs before each item is finalised.

## Purpose
1. Quietly check for likely cognitive bias
2. Read back the final SBI for confirmation
3. Capture cleanly

## Step 1 — Silent bias check

Evaluate the feedback against common bias patterns. **The agent does not announce this check.** If anything is flagged, surface it as a single, gentle question — not an audit.

### Patterns to check

**Halo / horn effect**
Is the feedback purely positive or purely negative across many dimensions, with no nuance?
Probe: *"Is there anything you've noticed that runs the other way? Even small."*

**Recency bias**
Are all examples from the last week or two?
Probe: *"Thinking back over a longer period, is there anything from earlier that still stands out?"*

**Character attribution**
Does the feedback still rest on what kind of person the recipient is, rather than what they did?
→ Return to skill 05 (Extracting observable behaviour) before proceeding.

**Attribution bias**
Is the provider attributing negative behaviour purely to character, without considering circumstances?
Probe: *"Do you have a sense of what might have been going on for them at the time?"*

**One-sided framing**
Is the feedback entirely about impact on the provider, with no consideration of broader context?
Probe: *"Did you notice how others experienced this — was it consistent with your reading?"*

**Affinity bias** (positive feedback variant of one-sided framing)
Is the provider giving consistently glowing feedback about someone who shares their background, communication style, or working preferences? Affinity often inflates positive evaluations.
Probe: *"What's something they could improve on, even small? Anyone has growth areas."*

**Intent inquiry** (an empathy prompt, not a bias category)
For developmental feedback, offering an intent-inquiry probe softens the verdict and produces more defensible feedback:
Probe: *"Do you have a sense of what they might have been trying to achieve in that moment?"*
This is not about excusing the behaviour — it builds nuance in the captured record.

**Language flagged for demographic bias** (system-level, not a probe)
Words like "aggressive", "abrasive", "shrill", "emotional", "bossy" are statistically applied unevenly across demographic groups for identical behaviour. If the feedback uses these and the recipient belongs to a group these are disproportionately applied to, return to skill 05 to extract the observable behaviour and let the captured record stand on what was actually done. Don't accuse the provider of bias; just probe for specifics.

### Rules for the bias check
- Pick the **strongest signal only** — don't stack multiple probes
- One probe, then move on regardless of the response
- If the provider declines to revise, capture the feedback as given — their judgment stands
- If no signal is strong, skip the bias check entirely and proceed to Step 2

## Step 2 — Final summary and confirmation

Read back the feedback as a clean SBI, in observable language only, and invite correction:

> Okay, here's what I've captured. [In/during situation], [recipient] [specific observable behaviour]. The effect was [impact]. Does that match what you wanted to share?

### Rules for confirmation
- Use the provider's language where possible
- Strip out evaluative or emotional language from the conversation
- If the feedback is anonymous and you haven't yet reviewed the "What anonymity does NOT do" section of skill 19, do so now
- Never store feedback without an explicit confirmation ("yes, that's right" or equivalent)
- If the provider corrects something, update and re-confirm

## Step 3 — Capture

Once confirmed, capture the feedback in the structured format:

- Situation (timestamped where possible)
- Behaviour (observable, neutral language)
- Impact (specific, grounded)
- Recipient
- Provider (or anonymised, per settings)
- Sentiment / category tags
- Specificity flag (high / medium / low)

Do not include emotionally charged or evaluative phrasing from the conversation — only the observable, agreed version.

## Important
- The bias check is light-touch. The agent is not auditing the provider; it's offering one final chance to broaden or recalibrate.
- The provider's autonomy is the priority. Their feedback, their judgment.
- Confirmation is not optional. Never store without explicit agreement.

## Interaction with other skills
- After capture, hand off to skill 16 to decide whether more items are coming
- If the provider declines to confirm, return to skill 07 or whichever sub-skill is needed to address the gap
