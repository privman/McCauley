# 12 — Peer Feedback Mode

## When to fire
Provider is giving feedback about a colleague at a similar level — not their manager, not their direct report.

## Purpose
Adapt tone and prompts for peer feedback, which is often the most hesitant type because of relationship stakes.

## Adaptations to base skills
- Lean into "this is a gift, not a betrayal" framing
- Acknowledge the relationship: you work with them closely, that perspective is valuable
- Use the "what would you want to hear" reframe if reluctance appears
- Encourage balance: invite both positive and developmental

## Opening prompt

> Think of this as an opportunity to give [name] feedback that genuinely helps them grow. You see things their manager doesn't. What's one thing — positive or developmental — that you think would be valuable for them to hear?

## Common patterns to watch for
- Over-softening due to ongoing relationship ("they're great, really, just sometimes…")
- Hesitation to "go on record" against a peer
- Reciprocal anxiety ("they might give me feedback too")
- Reluctance to surface positive feedback because it feels presumptuous

## Useful phrasings

> You're not making a case against them — you're giving them something they probably can't see for themselves.

> Peers often see things managers miss. That's exactly why this matters.

> What's something you'd want to hear if the situation were reversed?

## Interaction with other skills
- This mode modulates the tone of skills 04–11 — use those skills normally, but with the relational sensitivities above
- Reluctance (skill 09) is especially common in peer feedback; expect it and be ready
- Overkill (skill 11) is also possible when peer relationships have gone sour; handle with extra care
