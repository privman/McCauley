# Surface impact

Impact is what makes feedback worth giving. Without it, a behavior is just an observation.

## Three layers of impact

Useful impacts often live at one of:

1. **On the work**: "we shipped two weeks late", "the bug reached production", "we redesigned the routing because we'd missed that constraint"
2. **On other people**: "Sara stopped contributing in design reviews", "the on-call engineer was paged five times that night", "the junior engineers learned to ask Priya before trying things"
3. **On the team or org**: "trust between Mobile and Platform dropped", "we've adopted Alex's review style across the team", "leadership lost confidence in the roadmap"

## Probes

If impact is missing entirely:
- *"What was the consequence of that?"*
- *"What happened next?"*
- *"How did that affect the work / the team / you?"*

If impact is only emotional ("it was frustrating"):
- *"What did that frustration translate into — did it change what you did, or what you trusted them with?"*
- *"Beyond how it felt, what did it cost?"*

## Don't manufacture impact

If the user genuinely can't articulate an impact, the feedback may not be ready. Better to capture it as a draft and revisit than to invent consequences. Mark it incomplete and move on.
