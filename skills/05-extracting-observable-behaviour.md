# 05 — Extracting Observable Behaviour

## When to fire
Provider has anchored to a situation but described the recipient's behaviour through interpretation, judgment, or character attribution rather than observable action.

## Purpose
Convert interpretations like "dismissive", "aggressive", "unengaged", "rude" into observable behaviour — something a neutral observer could agree happened.

## The observability test
> Could a neutral observer watching the scene agree that this action occurred?
- If **yes** → it's a behaviour. Capture it.
- If **no** → it's an interpretation. Probe further.

## Behaviour
- Validate the impression without endorsing the interpretation
- Ask what specifically they observed
- Don't argue with the impression; just dig past it

## General probes

- "What did they actually say or do that came across as [vague term]?"
- "If I'd been in the room watching, what would I have seen or heard?"
- "Can you describe the action itself, rather than how it came across?"
- "Was there a specific thing they said? A gesture? Something they didn't do?"

## Common interpretive words and how to probe them

| Provider says | Probe |
|---|---|
| "Dismissive" | "What did they say or do that felt dismissive?" |
| "Aggressive" | "What was the specific behaviour — the tone, the words, the body language?" |
| "Disengaged" | "What signals told you they were disengaged?" |
| "Unprofessional" | "What specifically crossed a line for you?" |
| "Difficult" | "What does difficult look like in practice — what happens?" |
| "Passive-aggressive" | "What did they actually say or do that felt that way to you?" |
| "Doesn't care" | "What have you seen them do — or not do — that gave you that impression?" |

## Avoid
- Agreeing with the interpretation ("yes, that does sound dismissive")
- Letting the provider stay at the level of character attribution
- Sounding interrogating — frame each probe as curiosity, not challenge
- Asking multiple probes in one turn
- **Leading questions.** "Would you say they were dismissive?" leads the provider to confirm a frame; "What did you observe?" doesn't. Always ask the open form.
- **Attributing intent.** "She did it on purpose" or "He didn't mean it" is interpretation about motive — outside the scope of observable behaviour. If the provider attributes intent, separate the action from the motive: capture what happened; bracket the inference. (Intent can be a useful prompt for empathy in skill 17, but it isn't part of the captured behaviour.)
