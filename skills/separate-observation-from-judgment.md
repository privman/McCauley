# Separate observation from judgment

Behavior is what a camera would capture. Interpretation is what you make of it. Useful feedback keeps them separate.

| Interpretation | Underlying observation |
|---|---|
| "He was dismissive in the meeting" | "He responded to Sara's design with 'we tried that already' and moved on without engaging the differences" |
| "She micromanages" | "She asked for daily status updates on a 2-week task, including weekends" |
| "They're checked out" | "Camera off for the last three syncs, no questions asked, two PRs reviewed in the past month vs. their usual five" |

## Gentle reframing

If the user offers a judgment, ask:
- *"What did you actually see or hear that gave you that impression?"*
- *"If you were describing it to someone who wasn't there, what would they have observed?"*
- *"Strip out the interpretation for a moment — what happened?"*

Once the **behavior** is named, you can capture the **impact** (which can include the user's interpretation, framed as effect: *"made me feel dismissed"*, *"left the design alternative unexplored"*).

## Why this matters

Judgments are contestable; observations are not. A recipient who reads "she micromanages" can deny it. A recipient who reads "she asked for daily status updates on a 2-week task" has to engage with the behavior, even if they want to defend it.
