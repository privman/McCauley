# 02a — Positive Feedback Handling

## When to fire
The provider has signalled positive feedback — recognition, appreciation, or praise about something the recipient is doing well.

## Purpose
Positive feedback is the most under-cooked feedback in most organisations. Generic praise tells the recipient nothing specific to repeat. Treat positive feedback with the same structural rigour as developmental feedback: anchor it to a specific situation, name the observable behaviour, and explain the impact.

## Behaviour
- Don't accept "they're great" any more than you'd accept "they're terrible"
- Probe for the same SBI structure — what specifically, in what context, with what effect
- Acknowledge that capturing positive feedback well takes the same care as developmental
- Encourage the provider to surface positive feedback they might dismiss as "obvious" or "not worth noting"

## Example

A weak positive: *"Priya is a great communicator."*

A strong positive SBI:
- *Situation:* "In yesterday's client meeting"
- *Behaviour:* "Priya paused after presenting the proposal to ask the client directly what their main concern was"
- *Impact:* "This surfaced an objection we wouldn't have caught until the revision cycle, which saved us a week of rework"

## Probes for positive feedback

- "That's great to hear. Can you give me a specific example of when you noticed this most clearly?"
- "What did they actually do that had that effect?"
- "When was the most recent time this came up?"
- "What was the impact — on you, on the team, on the work?"

## Why this matters
- Recipients can only repeat behaviour they know to repeat. Vague praise produces no behaviour change.
- Specific positive feedback is rare and disproportionately valued by recipients.
- Encouraging positive feedback alongside developmental balances the record and reduces apparent bias toward criticism.

## Avoid
- Treating positive feedback as needing less rigour — it doesn't
- The "sandwich" pattern — wrapping developmental feedback between two pieces of positive feedback as a delivery tactic. Recipients see through it and remember only the praise. See skill 16.
- Pushing the provider to invent positive feedback they don't have — quality over quantity

## Interaction with other skills
- Runs in parallel with skills 04-07 — positive feedback still needs anchoring, observable behaviour, and impact
- Skill 17 (bias check) — for positive-only feedback, probe for halo effect: is there anything that runs the other way?
- Skill 16 (multi-feedback-sequencing) — if mixing positive and developmental in one session, capture as separate records, not as a sandwich
