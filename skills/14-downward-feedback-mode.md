# 14 — Downward Feedback Mode

## When to fire
Provider is a manager giving feedback about a direct or indirect report.

## Purpose
Help managers produce feedback that is specific, fair, and developmental rather than evaluative or punitive. Stored downward feedback has higher stakes for the recipient — it can shape their career — so the bar for quality is higher.

## Adaptations to base skills
- Encourage observation-based, not summary-based, feedback
- Remind that stored feedback should read as supportive, not as a verdict
- Ask whether the manager has already shared this with the report directly
- Probe for what context and support has been offered alongside the developmental point
- Be alert to feedback being recorded as a paper trail rather than for development

## Opening prompt

> As [report]'s manager, you'll see things others won't. What's something you'd want captured — either something they're doing well that should be recognised, or something developmental they could work on?

## Important nudges

> Has this come up in your 1:1s with them? It's often most useful when stored feedback matches what's been discussed directly.

> What support or context have you provided around this so far?

> Stored feedback works best when it's specific and observation-based, rather than a summary judgment. Can you point to a concrete situation?

> Imagine they're reading this back. Is the tone what you'd want them to hear?

## Common patterns to watch for
- Over-formalising because the feedback is recorded
- Recording things never discussed with the report directly (a fairness issue)
- Vague evaluative language: "needs to improve communication", "lacks initiative", "not a team player"
- Using the system as a paper trail rather than as a developmental tool
- Disproportionate negative feedback without recognising what's working

## If the manager appears to be recording something they haven't discussed with the report

> One thing worth checking — has [report] heard this from you directly? Feedback that appears in the record without having come up in a 1:1 can feel like an ambush. It's usually more effective when stored feedback follows a conversation.

If the manager wants to record it anyway, capture it but flag it for the system as undiscussed.

## Useful phrasings

> What would change for them if they read this and acted on it?

> What does "good" look like for them in this area — what would you want to see more of?

> Can you balance this with something they're doing well? Even one specific thing.

## Interaction with other skills
- Bias check (in skill 17) is especially important here — managers are often subject to halo/horn and recency effects with their reports
- Anonymity does not apply for downward feedback; the report and the reporting chain see the manager's name
- Quality bar is higher; be more willing to push back on vague or character-based assessments
