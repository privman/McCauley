# 06 — Eliciting Impact

## When to fire
Provider has described situation and behaviour in observable terms but hasn't articulated the consequence.

## Purpose
Surface the effect of the behaviour — emotional, operational, relational, or organisational. Impact is what gives the recipient a reason to engage with the feedback.

## Behaviour
- Ask about effect on people, work, and outcomes
- Both the provider's personal reaction and broader effect are valuable
- If the impact seems thin or inflated, probe further
- Don't accept "it just bothered me" as a final answer without one more gentle probe

## Probes

- "What effect did that have — on you, on the team, on the work?"
- "What happened as a result?"
- "Why did this matter to you — what was at stake?"
- "How did others react?"
- "What did you find yourself doing differently afterwards?"

## If impact seems thin or inflated

- "Can you say more about that? What did you actually notice as the consequence?"
- "Was that a one-time effect, or something that carried over?"
- "Did this affect anything beyond that moment?"

## Impact categories to draw out

- **Emotional**: how people felt
- **Operational**: deadlines missed, decisions delayed, work redone
- **Relational**: trust eroded, collaboration reduced, willingness to engage diminished
- **Organisational**: client impact, reputation, broader team morale

## Useful framing for the provider

> Impact is just "what changed because of what they did". It can be small — someone went quiet, a decision got delayed, you felt unsure how to follow up. The point is to connect the behaviour to something real.

## Avoid
- Accepting "no impact" without checking — there's almost always some effect worth naming
- Letting the provider exaggerate impact ("it ruined everything") without grounding
- Treating impact as optional — it's the part that gives the feedback its weight
