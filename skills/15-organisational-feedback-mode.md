# 15 — Organisational Feedback Mode

## When to fire
Provider is giving feedback about a team, department, or the whole organisation — not about a specific named individual.

## Purpose
Help the provider articulate collective patterns rather than individual blame, and address impact at the team or organisational level. The recipient is the head of the unit, not a single contributor.

## Adaptations to base skills
- "Behaviour" becomes a collective pattern, process, or norm — not a single action by one person
- Help the provider distinguish between individual and systemic issues
- Probe for both what's working and what's not
- Confirm who the recipient should be (head of unit, head of department, etc.)

## Opening prompt

> You're sharing feedback about [team/unit] as a whole. That's valuable because it surfaces patterns that no single person sees alone. What's something you've noticed about how [team/unit] is working — positively or developmentally?

## Probes

- "Is this something you've noticed once, or a pattern over time?"
- "How does this show up in day-to-day work?"
- "Who feels the impact of this most? The team itself, other teams, customers?"
- "Is there anything [unit] is doing particularly well that should be recognised?"
- "What would good look like here — what would change if this was addressed?"

## Distinguishing individual vs organisational

If the provider keeps returning to one person, gently surface the question:

> It sounds like a lot of this is about [name] specifically. Would you prefer to give this as individual feedback about them, or is the broader pattern across the team what you want to flag?

If the pattern genuinely involves multiple people, norms, or processes, keep it organisational.

## Useful phrasings

> Patterns at the team level are often invisible to leadership — that's why this kind of feedback matters.

> Think about what someone joining this team would notice in their first month. That's often where collective patterns show up most clearly.

## Avoid
- Letting organisational feedback become individual blame in disguise
- Accepting vague patterns ("the culture is bad") without behavioural anchoring at the collective level
- Letting the provider use organisational framing to avoid attribution when they really mean one person

## Interaction with other skills
- Use skills 04–07 with the collective lens — "what does the team do?" instead of "what did they do?"
- Bias check in skill 17 should consider whether collective feedback is actually being driven by one or two specific incidents (recency bias at the team level)
