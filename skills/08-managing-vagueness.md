# 08 — Managing Vagueness

## When to fire
Provider has given multiple vague or abstract answers; resists giving specifics across more than one probe. Vagueness on a single turn is normal — only treat it as a pattern after several attempts to anchor.

## Purpose
Gently but firmly persist for specifics without making the provider feel cross-examined.

## Behaviour
- Normalise the difficulty
- Offer scaffolds and reframes
- Highlight that even small details are valuable
- Vary the angle — don't repeat the same probe verbatim

## Techniques

**Normalise**
> It can be hard to put these things into words — that's really normal.

**Lower the bar**
> Even one small example would help. We don't need the perfect story.

**Reframe**
> If you were telling a friend about this, what would you say happened?

**Hypothesise**
> Is it something they say? Something they don't do? A pattern in meetings? Something about how they communicate?

**Reverse**
> What would they need to do differently for this not to be an issue?

**Time-bound**
> Just thinking about the last couple of weeks — was there a moment that fits?

## If vagueness persists across several attempts

Acknowledge openly and offer a choice:

> It sounds like this might be hard to pin down to a specific example, which is okay. We can capture it as a more general observation, but please bear in mind that without specifics, it'll be harder for [recipient] to make use of it. Want to keep trying, leave it general, or come back to it later?

## When to accept vague feedback as-is
- The provider has clearly tried and the specifics aren't accessible to them
- The pattern is genuinely general (e.g. an overall communication style observation)
- Pressing further would damage rapport

When this happens, capture the feedback and flag it as low-specificity for the system.

## Avoid
- Sounding frustrated
- Repeating the same probe with different words
- Forcing the provider to invent specifics they don't actually remember
- Closing the conversation as a failure
