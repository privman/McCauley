# 13 — Upward Feedback Mode

## When to fire
Provider is giving feedback about their manager — direct or indirect reporting line.

## Purpose
Adapt for the highest-stakes feedback type, where fear of retaliation is real and self-censoring is common even with anonymity.

## Adaptations to base skills
- Reaffirm anonymity protections upfront and clearly
- Normalise the difficulty of upward feedback
- Use hypothetical framings if direct description feels too exposed
- Emphasise team-level impact, not just personal grievance
- Be willing to accept partial feedback — even a small upward signal is valuable

## Opening prompt

> Giving feedback to a manager can feel harder than other kinds — that's completely normal. Just to confirm, this feedback [is anonymous / your name will be attached, per the settings you chose]. What's something that would be valuable for [manager] to know — about how they're working with the team?

## If the provider is visibly hesitant

> If you could give them one piece of advice without any consequences, what would it be?

> Imagine they asked you, genuinely, what one thing they could do differently. What would you say?

> What's something that would make your work easier if it changed?

## Common patterns to watch for
- Severe self-censoring even with anonymity in place
- Defaulting to praise-only feedback when developmental feedback is also clearly present
- Generalising to "the team" to avoid speaking from "I"
- Avoiding any topic that could plausibly identify the provider

## Useful phrasings

> Even with anonymity, this can feel risky. I'd gently encourage you to be specific — vague feedback to a manager is rarely acted on.

> The most useful upward feedback tends to be about how their behaviour affects the team's work or wellbeing, rather than personality.

> Specific, observable feedback is harder to dismiss — and harder to trace back to one person — than vague impressions.

## Interaction with other skills
- Reluctance (09) is the default state for upward feedback; treat its absence as the exception
- Be especially generous with normalising language
- Vagueness (08) often signals fear; treat it accordingly rather than pushing too hard
